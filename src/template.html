    Surface = WGS84 ellipsoid pushed in or out by the EGM96 geoid height (15′ grid, about 28 km). The real geoid spans only −107 m to +85 m, so relief is exaggerated by the factor shown. Coasts, borders and lakes: Natural Earth 1:50m. The geoid is a physical model of mean sea level, not a map of the seabed.
    Sea currents: simplified average paths of the main surface currents, drawn by hand; they are not measured flow. In the El Niño and La Niña views the two equatorial Pacific currents are slowed or sped up as an illustration.
    El Niño / La Niña: measured sea-surface temperature compared with the 1991–2020 average for the same months (NOAA ERSSTv5, 2° grid).
    Satellite orbits: altitudes and inclinations are to scale with the Earth, and time runs 600 times faster. The Earth does not turn in this view, so the rings stay fixed: in reality the ground track of a LEO or MEO satellite shifts west on each orbit, while a geostationary satellite stays above the same point. Satellite positions along each ring are illustrative, not live.
    Swarms: each Starlink, OneWeb and Planet dot stands for roughly four satellites, spread evenly over the shell as an illustration; the split between Starlink shells is approximate. The 25 Kinéis nanosatellites are all shown.
